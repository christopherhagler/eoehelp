How this works, and its limits

EoE reactions can take days to show, so a food counts toward a symptom day if you ate it that day or in the days just before, as set above. A day is only used when food was logged for it and for most of the days before it, because a day without a log is not a day without the food.

Each allergen group is compared with itself within each 4-week stretch, so a change of treatment, a slow flare, or a season is less likely to be mistaken for a food. A short flare inside one stretch can still be.

Nine groups are checked at once, and the bar for "more symptom days after" is raised to match, so a pattern that appears by chance is less likely to be reported as one. It can still happen.

A food counts for what its label declares and the ingredients it lists. "May contain" warnings are not counted. A dish like mayonnaise counts for the groups its usual recipe contains, but recipes vary, so the label of the product you actually ate is more exact.

Most foods will read "can't tell yet". A food diary can only show strong, consistent patterns, and symptoms don't always follow inflammation. Supervised elimination and reintroduction with your gastroenterologist, confirmed by biopsy, is how food triggers are actually established.
